Funding is not free money: what a −1% print costs a short

This morning the scanner flagged six contracts with negative funding. The most extreme was BERA at −1.112% per 4 hours, which the scanner printed as −2436% annualised. The number looks like a gift if you are short. It is not. Converted into what it actually charges a position, it is one of the most expensive trades on the board.

1. The conversion everyone skips

Funding is quoted per period — usually 4 hours, sometimes 1 hour. To turn it into a daily cost, multiply by the number of periods in a day. To annualise, multiply by that many again and by 365.

So a −1.112% / 4h print costs a short:

  • 1.112% × 6 periods = 6.67% per day
  • 6.67% × 365 = 2436% per year

On a $1,000 short, that is about $66.72 a day paid to the other side — before you have made or lost a cent on price.

3. Why the crowd still does it

Because when these prints appear, price often does move fast. AKE was up 256% on the day. A short that catches 10% of that pays the funding many times over. The mistake is not taking the trade; it is staying in it after the move has happened, when the only thing left is the carry.

This is the same mechanism behind the squeeze I wrote about in three days of INIT: shorts crowd in, funding goes deeply negative, shorts pay longs every few hours, and the first leg up forces the weakest shorts to buy back — which is the second leg up. The funding print is not the signal. It is the receipt for who is already trapped.

4. What I actually do with a print like this

  • Compute the daily carry first. If it is above ~2% per day, the trade must work within hours, not days.
  • Check whether price already moved. The scanner shows the 24h move next to the funding — a −1% print after a +40% day is a different object from a −1% print on a flat chart.
  • Size for the carry, not for the stop. A position that can bleed 6% a day needs to be small enough that two wrong days do not end the month.
  • Exit on the funding normalising. When the print comes back toward neutral, the crowd has thinned out and the carry no longer pays you for the risk.
Extreme funding is not a direction. It is a price for waiting. The scanner flags it because it changes the cost structure of a position — not because it knows where the price goes next.
