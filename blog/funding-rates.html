How to read funding rates and open interest (without fooling yourself)

Most altcoin traders look at price and nothing else. Price tells you what happened; funding and open interest tell you who is behind it. Here is the short version I use on my own screen.

1. Funding rate: the crowd pays the patient side

Perpetual futures have no expiry date, so exchanges use a periodic payment between longs and shorts to keep the contract near spot. Positive funding means longs pay shorts. Negative means shorts pay longs.

That single number answers a question price cannot: which side is crowded?

Funding (per 8h)ReadingWhat it tends to precede
+0.05% or moreLongs crowded and payingStalling price gets sold; late longs become exit liquidity
+0.01% to +0.05%Mild long biasNothing on its own
-0.01% to -0.05%Mild short biasNothing on its own
-0.05% or lowerShorts crowded and payingBounces can squeeze violently; shorting here is late

Two practical details people miss. First, funding intervals differ — many majors settle every 8 hours, but some contracts settle every 1 or 4 hours, so compare the daily cost, not the per-settlement number. Second, a hot funding rate is a warning about positioning, not a signal to enter. If you short purely because funding is positive, you will get stopped out by the squeeze that often comes first.

2. Price + open interest: real money or closed positions

Open interest is the total number of open contracts. Price tells you the direction; OI tells you whether new positions were created or old ones were closed.

PriceOpen interestReading
UpUpNew longs entering — the move has fuel, but chasing is late
UpDownShort covering — mechanical, tends to fade
DownUpNew shorts entering — the move can extend
DownDownLongs stopping out — selling may be near exhaustion

This is the difference between a move that keeps going and one that snaps back the moment the last short is covered. A 5-minute spike with flat OI is usually noise; the same spike with OI jumping is someone with size.

3. Beta vs own weakness

Compare every coin to ETH over the same window. If ETH is flat and the coin is down 5%, that is the coin's own problem — an unlock, a supply increase, a delisting notice, a project issue. If ETH is down 5% and the coin is down 5%, that is beta, and it tells you nothing about the coin.

Beta is noise. Own weakness is information.

This one distinction filters out most of the fake "signals" in a red market. When everything is down, most of what you see is the market, not the coin.

4. What this does not do

None of this predicts price. It describes the balance of positions and tells you when the crowd is on the wrong side of its own trade. You still need an entry, a stop, and a size that lets you be wrong without drama.
